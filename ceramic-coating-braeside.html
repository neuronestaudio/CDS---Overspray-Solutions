Beside an industrial belt

What Braeside does to a car’s finish.

Industrial fallout

Airborne metal particle lands on the bonnet, roof and boot and corrodes into the clear coat as tiny orange specks. It is dissolved with an iron treatment, then clayed out.

Workshop overspray

Spray booths and fabrication shops release paint mist that drifts onto cars in nearby car parks. It bonds hard and is removed without cutting the paint down.

Heavy-vehicle grime

Trucks and machinery leave an oily diesel film on everything around them. It makes a car look dirty two days after a wash because it re-sticks to bare paint.

Work utes and vans

Vehicles that work on site take dust, spatter and chemical splashes. Coating them makes the end-of-day clean far quicker.

FAQs

Before you book from Braeside.

Can the work be done at my place in Braeside?

Andy is mobile as well as studio-based, so the work can be done at your place in Braeside or at the Berwick studio, about 20 km away. Work vehicles and fleets can be done at your workplace or depot so they are not off the road for a drop-off; private cars at home or in the studio, whichever is easier. Tell us where the car is kept when you book and you will get a straight answer on which suits the job.

Is fallout the same as overspray?

No. Fallout is metal particle that corrodes into the clear coat; overspray is paint or similar material that bonds on top. They are removed differently, which is why we assess before touching the paint.

Can fallout damage be permanent?

If it is left long enough to pit the clear coat, some marks may need correction and a few can be too deep to remove fully. Caught early, it comes out cleanly.

Why does my paint feel rough after a wash?

That is almost always bonded industrial fallout or overspray. A wash slides over it; it has to be removed chemically and with a clay treatment.
